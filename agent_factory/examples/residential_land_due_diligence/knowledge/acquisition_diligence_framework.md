# Residential Land Acquisition Diligence Framework

Start by separating fatal flaws from normal diligence work. The goal is not to prove every risk is zero. The goal is to identify whether the parcel can realistically move from acquisition to lot delivery inside the buyer's capital, schedule, and return requirements.

Core diligence buckets:
- Entitlement and zoning
- Utilities and agency capacity
- Title, access, and survey
- Environmental and civil/site constraints
- Contract structure and seller deliverables
- Market fit and lot yield economics

Recommended sequence:
1. Check zoning posture, entitlement status, and whether annexation or rezone is required.
2. Confirm access, title exceptions, and whether the tract has defensible legal frontage.
3. Validate sewer, water, drainage, and fire flow conditions with the agencies.
4. Screen for wetlands, floodplain, remediation, endangered species, and abnormal geotech risk.
5. Review likely lot yield, offsite obligations, impact fees, and schedule exposure.
6. Make sure the purchase agreement preserves enough time and control for diligence.

Healthy signs:
- By-right or already entitled residential land.
- Existing utilities or credible will-serve capacity.
- Clean title with straightforward access.
- Limited offsite improvement scope.
- Strong fit with the builder's product and price point.

Escalation signs:
- Multiple major risks stacking at once.
- Unclear access or title defects.
- Utility delivery that depends on someone else solving a corridor-scale problem.
- Environmental issues that may materially reduce lot yield or extend timing.
- Contract terms that force hard money before the key unknowns are resolved.
