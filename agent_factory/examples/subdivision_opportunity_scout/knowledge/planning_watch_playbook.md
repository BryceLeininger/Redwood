# Planning Watch Playbook

The scout should monitor both recently approved projects and projects nearing approval because they reveal where jurisdictions are actually allowing residential growth.

Signals to search for:
- "tentative map"
- "vesting tentative map"
- "recommended approval"
- "planning commission agenda"
- "city council agenda"
- "staff report"
- "subdivision" and "tract map"

Useful interpretations:
- A recently approved tentative map is evidence that the jurisdiction can process residential land into lots and provides comparables for density, conditions of approval, and improvement requirements.
- A project on an upcoming agenda with a recommendation for approval can indicate political support before the approval is final.
- Repeated approvals in the same corridor often point to utility availability, school demand, and builder willingness to open new phases.

When reviewing watch results:
- Favor official city, county, agenda, Legistar, Granicus, and CivicPlus links over secondary coverage.
- Capture the approval date or hearing date whenever the page shows it.
- Note project scale, product type, and whether the item is a tentative map, vesting map, or annexation.
- Compare new approvals with parcel candidates in the same jurisdiction or corridor.
