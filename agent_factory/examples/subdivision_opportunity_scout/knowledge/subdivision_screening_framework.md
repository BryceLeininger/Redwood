# Residential Subdivision Screening Framework

Start with fatal flaws before underwriting upside. The first screen is whether the land can realistically be platted into residential lots within the capital and timing constraints of the buyer.

Key positives:
- By-right or clearly aligned residential zoning.
- Tentative map or vesting tentative map already approved.
- Utilities at site or stubbed nearby with confirmed capacity.
- Adjacency to existing subdivisions, finished lots, or recent builder activity.
- Growth corridor location with clear demand from entry-level or move-up product.
- Manageable site work, straightforward frontage, and limited offsite obligations.

Key risks:
- Floodplain, wetlands, endangered species, or remediation problems.
- Annexation, rezone, septic dependency, or major utility extension needs.
- Steep topography that drives grading, retaining walls, or poor lot efficiency.
- Title, access, easement, or litigation issues.
- High impact fees or heavy offsite improvement scope.
- Entitlement timelines that drift beyond the buyer's hold period.

Scoring guidance:
- Prioritize parcels that combine zoning clarity, utility readiness, builder demand, and low civil complexity.
- Watch parcels that have demand and location strength but still need one or two major diligence items resolved.
- Pass on parcels where environmental, utility, title, or entitlement risk overwhelms lot value upside.
