# Environmental, Title, And Site Risk Signals

These are the issues that most often move a land deal from workable to unfinanceable or too slow.

Environmental red flags:
- Wetlands or waters of the U.S.
- Floodplain or major drainage burden.
- Endangered species habitat.
- Brownfield or remediation concerns.
- CEQA or NEPA scope that materially changes timing.

Title and access red flags:
- Unclear legal access.
- Easements that interfere with loting or improvements.
- Title defects, liens, or unresolved ownership issues.
- Litigation tied to access, boundaries, or development rights.

Site and civil red flags:
- Steep grading or expensive retaining walls.
- Uncertain pad yield.
- Major offsite improvements.
- Poor frontage, awkward shape, or constrained ingress/egress.
- Fire flow or drainage obligations that break the basis.

How to interpret these issues:
- One manageable issue may only require targeted follow-up.
- Two or three major issues together often create fatal flaw risk.
- The real question is not whether the issue exists, but whether it can be solved inside the planned hold period and land basis.

Practical discipline:
- Ask what each issue does to lot count, timeline, and land cost.
- Separate risks that are quantifiable from risks that are still conceptual.
- If the seller cannot produce credible backup, assume the risk is worse than marketed.
