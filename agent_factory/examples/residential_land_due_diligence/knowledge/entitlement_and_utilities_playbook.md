# Entitlement And Utilities Playbook

Entitlement diligence should answer two questions:
- What approvals are already in hand?
- What major approvals are still uncertain, political, or time-consuming?

Best-positioned land for a homebuilder:
- By-right single-family zoning.
- Tentative map or vesting tentative map already approved.
- Final map or finished lots when speed to market matters.
- Conditions of approval that are understandable and financeable.

Higher-risk entitlement setups:
- Annexation before processing can even start.
- Rezone or general plan amendment with no clear political support.
- Moratoria, utility allocation queues, or unresolved agency timing.
- Density assumptions that are not yet grounded in agency feedback.

Utilities diligence should focus on actual deliverability, not optimistic seller language.

Questions to close:
- Is sewer physically nearby and does the agency have capacity?
- Is water service available at the right pressure and timing?
- Are storm drain and fire flow upgrades routine or exceptional?
- Are there reimbursement, oversizing, or backbone obligations?

Useful evidence:
- Will-serve letters.
- Recent agency emails or meeting notes.
- Improvement plans or utility studies.
- Nearby subdivisions that prove capacity and timing.

Warning signs:
- "Utilities nearby" without capacity confirmation.
- Service depending on a future regional project.
- Unknown lift station, booster, or oversized backbone cost.
- Agency comments that remain verbal and undocumented.
