# Contract And Closing Checklist For Residential Land Deals

Good land diligence can still fail if the purchase agreement forces the buyer to absorb risk too early.

Key contract items to review:
- Diligence period length and extension rights.
- Access rights for survey, environmental, soils, and civil consultants.
- Seller obligation to deliver title, survey, studies, approvals, and utility information.
- Earnest money timing and when deposits go hard.
- Cure rights for title objections and access defects.
- Closing conditions tied to approvals, lot yield, utilities, or annexation milestones.

Healthy structure:
- Enough time to finish the highest-risk diligence tasks.
- Clear seller cooperation language.
- Hard money only after the biggest unknowns are resolved.
- Ability to terminate if title, access, utilities, or approvals do not clear.

Weak structure:
- Quick hard money with incomplete diligence.
- Heavy reliance on verbal seller statements.
- Limited access for third-party consultants.
- No remedy if utility or title assumptions fail.

Closing mindset:
- The contract should match the risk profile of the land.
- Near-finished lots can tolerate a tighter structure.
- Raw or politically uncertain land needs more time, conditions, and control.
